The basics (Australian edition)

1. Emergency fund first

Keep 3 months of expenses in a high-interest savings account (~5.25%+ right now). It grows every month and never drops. That's the part you'll see go up steadily.

2. Shares go up… eventually

Broad share ETFs have historically returned about 7–10% a year, but not in a straight line. Expect some red weeks and the occasional 20–35% drop. Over 10+ years the line has trended up. Selling in a panic is the #1 way people lose money.

3. Pick a CHESS-sponsored broker

CHESS sponsorship means the shares are registered in your name. Popular low-cost options for ETFs include CMC Invest, Webull, Stake, Moomoo, Pearler and CommSec. Betashares Direct is $0 brokerage but custodial.

4. $500 minimum

The ASX requires at least $500 for your first purchase of any ETF or share. After that you can top up with any amount.

5. Tax perks

Franking credits: Aussie company dividends come with tax already paid, so you may get some back. CGT discount: hold for 12+ months and only half your gain is taxed. Dividends are taxable income, so keep records (the "My money" tab helps).

6. Automate it

Adding a fixed amount every month ("dollar-cost averaging") means you buy more when prices are low. It also stops you trying to time the market. Most people who try to time the market do worse.

7. Don't forget super

Super is already invested for you. Check your fund's option (e.g. "High Growth" if you're young) and fees. Extra contributions can save tax, but the money is locked away until retirement.

8. Red flags

Anyone guaranteeing returns, "signals" groups on Telegram/Facebook, crypto "mentors", pressure to act fast. These are scam patterns. Check Scamwatch.
